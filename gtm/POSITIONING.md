# Positioning, second pass

The first version led with the morpheme breakdown: 보고 싶다 is 보 plus 고 plus
싶. That was the wrong headline, for two reasons.

It is a feature, not a reason to click. It only lands on someone who has already
decided to study Korean grammar, which is a small and well served audience.

And it is not ours. mirinae.io parses Korean the same way. btslyrictranslations
writes grammar posts. Leading with it put the commodity on the sign and left the
thing nobody else has in the basement.

## What is actually ours

140 culture and idiom notes across 35 songs. They decode lines that a Korean
hears instantly and a non-Korean has never had access to, because the English
translation flattens them.

The fan has been singing these lines for years. They do not know what they said.
That gap is the product and it is also the hook.

### The strongest ones, straight from the data

| Song | Line | What a Korean hears |
|---|---|---|
| MIC Drop | 우리 콘서트 절대 없어 포도 | 포도 is a grape, and it is ticketing slang. Unsold seats show as purple dots on Interpark's seat map, so a chart full of grapes means a show that did not sell. The line is a boast: we never have them. Every English version says grape. |
| 뱁새 | 그 말하는 넌 뭔 수저길래 | 수저 is the spoon you were born holding. 금수저 inherited money, 흙수저 none. The English title Silver Spoon half-translates it and loses the argument. |
| 쩔어 | 3포세대? 5포세대? | 포 is 포기, giving up. The 삼포 generation gave up dating, marriage and children; 오포 added a home and a career. A whole social diagnosis in four syllables. |
| IDOL | 얼쑤 좋다 / 지화자 좋다 | 추임새, the shouts an audience throws at a pansori singer to push them on. Not ad libs. |
| IDOL | 덩기덕 쿵더러러 | 구음, mouth notation for janggu drum strokes, the way a drummer says boom bap. |
| 쩔어 | 모로 가도 달리는 성공가도 | Half a proverb: 모로 가도 서울만 가면 된다, go sideways if you like as long as you reach Seoul. Every Korean finishes it silently. |
| MIC Drop | 혹 배가 아프다면 고소해 | 배가 아프다 is a stomach ache and the standard idiom for envy. 고소해 then means sue me, it tastes good, and serves you right, all at once. |
| Serendipity | 넌 내 푸른곰팡이 | Penicillium, the mould Fleming found on a neglected dish. In Korean school science it is the stock example of a lucky accident. Calling someone that is not an insult. |
| Blood Sweat & Tears | He too was a tempter | Lifted from Hesse's Demian, where a friend is a door into a second, forbidden world. |
| Spring Day | 홀로 남은 설국열차 | 설국열차 is the Korean title of Snowpiercer. |

## The new line

**You have been singing these songs for years. You have no idea what you said.**

Then: 35 BTS songs, decoded the way a Korean hears them. Every line, every
reference, every piece of slang that the English translation quietly deleted.

The grammar layer stays in the product and moves out of the headline. It is the
answer to "so how do I read the next one myself", which is the second question,
not the first.

## Why this converts better

A grammar breakdown asks the viewer to want to study. A decode asks them to
feel caught out about a song they love, which is a much lower bar and a much
stronger impulse. It also makes them want to tell another fan, because being the
one who knows is the currency in a fandom.

And it is defensible. Anyone can ship a Korean parser. Nobody else has sat
through 35 songs writing down what 포도 means on a seat map.

## What to lead with per channel

- **Reels and Shorts**: one decode, one reveal, under 20 seconds. 포도 is the
  best opener because it is self contained, funny, and provably invisible to a
  non-Korean. 수저 is the strongest second.
- **Carousel**: three decodes from one song, so the slide count earns itself.
- **Reddit r/bangtan**: the list format. "Ten lines where the English translation
  dropped something." This is a post fans actually want, not an ad.
- **X**: one decode per post, reply into threads about that song.

## One thing to not use

Spring Day's Sewol reading is in the app and belongs there. It should not be the
hook in a promotional clip. Using a disaster that killed 250 schoolchildren to
stop a scroll is the wrong trade, whatever it would do for reach.
